I study energetic plasma environments across vastly different scales — from the Galactic Center of the Milky Way to megaparsec-scale galaxy clusters. My goal is to understand how shocks, turbulence, and magnetic fields shape high-energy emission and particle acceleration in extreme astrophysical systems.

## Galactic Center

  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/the_GC_Chimney.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="X-ray map of the Galactic Center, with labeled northern and southern chimneys of hot gas around Sagittarius A*" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Diffuse X-ray emission in the Galactic Center. Credit: Gabriele Ponti/MPE/INAF and Mark Morris/UCLA.</figcaption>

The Galactic Center (GC) is the nearest laboratory for studying feedback, particle acceleration, and diffuse X-ray emission in a dense, magnetized environment.

My research focuses on:

- Diffuse X-ray structures in the Central Molecular Zone
- Thermal and non-thermal plasma diagnostics
- Shock interactions with molecular clouds
- Energy partition between plasma and cosmic rays

Using deep observations from *Chandra* and *XMM-Newton*, I perform spatially resolved spectroscopy and multi-wavelength analysis to trace the origin and evolution of energetic structures in the GC.

## Galaxy Clusters

Galaxy clusters are the largest gravitationally bound systems in the Universe. Their hot intracluster medium (ICM) provides a unique environment to study plasma physics on cosmological scales.

My work investigates:

- Temperature and entropy structure of the ICM
- Merger-driven shocks and cold fronts
- Particle acceleration in cluster environments
- Coupling between thermal plasma and relativistic particles

By combining X-ray and radio observations, I aim to understand how energy is transported and redistributed during cluster mergers.

## Methods

- High-resolution X-ray imaging and spectroscopy
- Multi-wavelength data integration
- Statistical modeling and uncertainty quantification
- Python-based scalable data pipelines
